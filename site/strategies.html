Start Here

Location gives the setup. Context gives permission. Price gives the trigger.

That distinction keeps a beginner from turning every indicator or confluence into a buy/sell signal.

01

Location

Where is price reacting? ORB high/low/midpoint, prior-day levels, Asia/London extremes, equal highs/lows, FVGs, or another mapped liquidity area.

Question: Are we at a meaningful place?
02

Context

What is the market doing around that location? Higher-timeframe direction, session behavior, VWAP/EMA alignment, ES/NQ relationship, news risk, and whether price is trending or chopping.

Question: Does the environment support the idea?
03

Trigger

What makes the trade actionable now? Reclaim, rejection, displacement, market-structure shift, engulfing body, wick rejection, or a clean retest response.

Question: Did price actually confirm?
04

Invalidation

Where is the thesis wrong? A failed reclaim, acceptance back through the level, opposite-side structure break, or the predefined stop/risk boundary.

Question: What proves the trade wrong?

Confluence Dictionary

What each common confirmation is actually supposed to do.

Confluence is useful when each piece has a job. Stacking five indicators that all say the same thing is not five independent reasons.

4H / 1H structureBias

Frames whether the larger auction is trending, reversing, or balanced. It should stop a tiny lower-timeframe signal from becoming the whole thesis.

Session highs / lowsLocation / target

Objective references for where stops and resting orders may cluster. Useful for both setup location and plausible destination.

VWAPContext

Intraday fair-value / participation reference. Used to judge whether price is accepting above or below the session’s volume-weighted mean.

EMA stackMomentum context

Shorter/longer moving averages help describe trend alignment. They are not an entry without price confirming at a meaningful location.

FVG / imbalanceLocation

Marks an area of fast one-sided movement that may later be revisited. Its role is to create a place to watch, not a mandatory reversal.

Liquidity sweepSetup condition

Shows price has traded through an obvious high/low. A sweep matters more when price then fails to accept beyond it.

MSS / displacementConfirmation

Evidence that short-term control may have shifted. Stronger when it appears after the intended liquidity event or retest.

Reclaim / rejection candleTrigger

The final price response at the level: close back through, defend, engulf, or reject. This is closer to the actual “click” than the context layers above it.
