Current Strategy Framework

Mason ORB.

An evolving MES / ES New-York-morning framework built from session liquidity, a local opening range, acceptance / rejection, retest behavior, price-action confirmation, and the next plausible liquidity objective. The current goal is not to add more concepts — it is to identify which parts of the process actually carry edge.

The Process

How the morning is actually read.

The ORB is a reference inside a larger map. The model starts with where liquidity sits and how price behaves around those references.

01Map Asia, London, and previous-day liquidity
02Build ORB high, low, midpoint, and range
03Read pre-New-York breaks, sweeps, and acceptance
04Wait for the NY-open participation shift
05Use retest / reclaim / rejection instead of chasing
06Stack the evidence that is actually present
07Target the next plausible liquidity objective
What We Have Tested

The framework has a real history.

These are not all the same strategy, which is exactly why they are useful: each version changed the question and exposed a different weakness or strength.

V1 study38 trades

Simple baseline looked promising enough to continue

Reconstructed sample: PF 1.78, +$2,381.25 net, +$62.66 expectancy/trade, 31.6% win rate. Good enough for forward-testing research, not large enough for production confidence.

R1.282 trades

More trades did not automatically improve the system

The adaptive second-trade branch reached 43.9% win rate but finished at PF 0.949 and -$203.75. That version is useful negative evidence against equating activity or win rate with edge.

R1.374 reported

Retest + confluence moved back positive

MES 5-minute export: PF 1.284 and +$546.25. It remains research-grade because the total-trade count does not reconcile with the listed wins and losses.

V6156 trades

Midpoint continuation became the strongest historical export

ES 15-minute export: PF 1.431, +$32,750, 39.1% win rate, 17.75% max drawdown. It is the best current large-sample historical result in the comparison file, not a live track record.

Current Research Stack

What is solid enough to use, and what still needs proof.

Session highs / lowsObjective

Asia, London, and previous-day references can be measured cleanly and stored as session state.

ORB high / low / midpointTesting

The levels are objective; the preferred clock remains a controlled research question because 8:00–8:15 and cash-open variants represent different market regimes.

Retest / acceptance / rejectionTesting

Central to the human process and historical variants. The next strategy harness needs matched comparisons against immediate breakout entries.

VWAP / EMA / displacementAttribution pending

Implemented or historically used, but not independently proven as required filters.

FVG / imbalance contextDiscretionary

Useful chart context in the current process; not yet a locked mechanical requirement.

Liquidity target selectionDiscretionary

Candidate levels can be measured; deciding which objective price is seeking still needs structured labels and outcome data.

Next Tests

What gets answered when Pine research resumes.

ORB clock isolation

8:00–8:15 versus cash-open variants on matched dates, timeframe, risk, costs, and exits.

Entry-mode attribution

Immediate breakout versus boundary retest versus midpoint retest.

Filter attribution

VWAP, EMA, displacement, range, volume, and other filters measured one change at a time.

Risk / exit reconciliation

Lock costs, position sizing, stop logic, target model, break-even behavior, and forced-flat timing before comparisons.

Forward evidence

Replay, paper-forward, and eventually funded/live evidence stored separately from backtests.
