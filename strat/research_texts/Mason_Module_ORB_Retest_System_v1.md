Quick Map

1. Where Hayes' Style Ends and Mason's Style Begins

Hayes' Discord material is a broad trading education folder. It covers futures, options, gold, liquidity, ICT concepts, market structure, candlesticks, psychology, journaling, and backtesting. That folder is good for learning the language of trading.

Mason's module is not trying to replace that foundation. It is meant to give people a second, more mechanical route: a simple futures model that can be repeated, tested, and journaled without guessing all day.

3. Daily Market Prep Checklist

Do this before considering any trade. This keeps the session from turning into random clicking.

☐ Check economic calendar: CPI, PPI, FOMC, NFP, Powell/Fed speakers, major 10:00 a.m. reports.

☐ Mark prior day high, prior day low, overnight high, overnight low, and obvious liquidity pools.

☐ Mark 4H and 1H directional context: are we making higher highs/higher lows, lower highs/lower lows, or chopping?

☐ Mark the 8:00-8:15 ORB high, low, and midpoint.

☐ Mark VWAP and 20 EMA on the active chart. Note whether price is above/below and whether they are stacked cleanly.

☐ Check ES/NQ relationship. If trading NQ/MNQ/MES, confirm the broader index movement is not fighting your idea.

☐ Write today's bias in one sentence before market open. Example: 'Bullish only above ORB high if VWAP holds; no shorts unless ORB low fails.'

☐ Decide max loss before the trade. Do not decide risk after emotion shows up.

4. Mason's Main Setup: 8:00-8:15 ORB Retest

The setup starts before the regular market open. The 8:00-8:15 ORB gives a clean reference range. The trade is not the first breakout by itself. The trade is the controlled retest after the market shows direction.

5. Confluence Stack: Permission vs. Trigger

A major beginner mistake is treating every confluence like an entry signal. In this system, confluences give permission. The actual trigger is still price reacting at the ORB retest level.
