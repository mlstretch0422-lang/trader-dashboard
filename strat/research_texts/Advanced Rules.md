Rules for Options


Disclaimer; You CAN ONLY trade options on Main Market Sessions, there’s no trading them on Afterhours or Pre-Market… This is not Futures. This means you must trade right at market open for maximum volume.

Options are contracts in which you can leverage your position 100x its normal value. When trading, you buy contracts, each contract represents 100 shares of that stock. When buying a contract it’ll show a price like “ASK: 4.66 and 4.55 BID,” when buying, you always click on the ASK, and those numbers represent the premium. Always multiply this number by 100 to determine the true price of one of these contracts. That would mean for this example 1 contract would cost you 466$. Each contract will have a different price depending on how close it is to the stock price, what I’m about to explain is a little confusing so bare with me:


Let’s say we’re trading QQQ and the stock price is 600 for this example:

Calls (option contracts go up in value when stock price goes up) - buying a call contract at 600 strike price would be considered ATM (at the money) - buying a call contract at 601 strike price would be considered ITM (in the money) - buying a call contract at 599 strike price would be considered OTM (out of the money)

*ITM contracts are more expensive then OTM contracts, but OTM contracts allow more leveraging (more percent), but it’s obviously riskier

Puts (option contracts go up in value when stock price goes down) - buying a put contract at 600 strike price is ATM - buying a put contract at 601 would be considered OTM - buying a put contract at 599 would be considered ITM, so it’s basically flipped.

There are many different strategies to utilize options, but as day traders we are interested in 1 thing only; Buying Calls or Puts for entry, then eventually selling those same positions for exit. Calls are when you believe price to rise, and Puts are the opposite. Options allow EXTREME leveraging for HIGH returns, but unless you’re disciplined and time your entries/exits perfectly, you will be wiped…
To trade options, you have to keep in mind the 3 greek letters or “built in rules” that all option contracts follow. 

About Greek Symbols, et cetera: There are 3 main greek symbols that you have to worry about…

Theta: This greek symbol measures time decay, every single option has an expiration date and as time passes your position bleeds, meaning you lose percentage. Keeping Theta decay in your mind before trading is VITAL. Option contracts come in all different time frames, meaning that you can buy contracts that expire next friday, next month, next year, or even at the end of that same day, which is what we do. Now one thing to keep in mind is that the further out you buy, the more expensive those contracts are. (Ex if I buy 5 contracts to expire in 365 days, it’s much more expensive then buying 5 contracts that expire next week)

Delta: This greek symbol measures how much the option contract price will move for every dollar that the stock goes up or down. ITM option contracts have more Delta then OTM option contracts. Even with this being the case, OTM contracts still make more percentage than ITM contracts because OTM contracts are much cheaper.

Gamma: This is technically important, this determines how much Delta will move if stock goes up and down. Seems like a lot, but it’s basically irrelevant, I guarantee you this will do you no good to keep in mind. 

Vega

*Most important ones you have to keep in mind is Theta and Delta, but as you get more experienced, you won’t even have to look at these, you’ll already know what your contracts delta is based on how close your strike price is to the middle (current stock price) and Theta also tends to stay consistent*

Another note about Theta: Especially when buying 0 DTE options, the rate you lose percentage will increase and actually compound the longer you wait. When there’s still 6 hours left of market open, you’ll lose about 10% on your position every hour, when there’s 3 hours left of markets, you’ll lose about 20% every hour, and if you are for some reason still in the trade and there’s 2 or 1 hour left of market open, just quit options and go to futures, you’re practically completely liquidated on your contract/s. This along should tell you so much about options, unless you’re buying 3 or 4 DTE, you shouldn’t be taking long intraday trades. As option traders, we’re scalpers… The most they should last would be 30 min (MOST).

Trades are within the first 30 minutes to 1 hour of market open, unless some news comes out or some kind of catalyst for strong direction allows you to trade later, but always trade strong volume

Never trade chop, 

Have a stop loss set even before getting into the trade and at least have a level for exit, but remember, sometimes it’s better to get out faster, especially if the direction is dying (oversold, overbought)
