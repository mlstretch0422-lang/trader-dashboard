# MASON'S ACTUAL TRADING DECISION PROCESS
## Raw Source Material - Not a Rulebook
**This document captures how Mason actually thinks while trading, before categorizing into rules/hypotheses/framework.**

---

## 1. PRE-MARKET ANALYSIS (Before 9:30 ET)

### **Session Structure Mapping**
Mason's first step is NOT looking at ORB breakouts.

He first establishes market structure by identifying:
- **Asia session:** High, low, consolidation zones
- **London session:** High, low, consolidation zones  
- **Previous day:** High, low, key reference points

These appear as colored zones with extending reference lines on his indicator.

**Purpose:** Before New York opens, establish:
1. Consolidation area
2. Directional bias
3. Potential liquidity targets
4. Reference points for structure
5. Midpoints
6. Session highs/lows as potential targets

**Question Mason asks:** "What structure did the overnight sessions create? Where might price be drawn?"

**Classification:** 
- Objective: Session highs and lows can be measured
- Discretionary: Determining "which liquidity target is most likely" requires judgment

---

### **8:00–8:15 ORB Formation**

After establishing session structure, Mason marks the 8:00–8:15 range.

**This is DIFFERENT than "trade after the range closes."**

Purpose of the 8:00–8:15 ORB:
- Establish opening structure
- Create reference points (high, low, midpoint)
- Understand initial participation/volume
- Set up bias

**NOT automatic entry trigger.** It's a reference point for further analysis.

**Classification:**
- Objective: 8:00–8:15 range high/low can be measured
- Discretionary: Whether to act on it depends on post-range behavior

## 2. PRE-MARKET BEHAVIOR (8:15–9:30 ET)

### **Watching for Direction Before New York Opens**

Between range close (8:15) and NY open (9:30), Mason observes:

**Examples of pre-NY behavior:**
- Does price immediately move higher? (bullish signal)
- Does price immediately move lower? (bearish signal)
- Does price stay neutral/consolidate? (indecision)
- Has a first breakout already occurred? (information)

**Mason's thought process:**
"By the time New York opens, price has already revealed something about this day's direction."

This pre-NY behavior modifies his thesis BEFORE he considers entry.

**Measurable elements:**
- Direction of pre-NY movement relative to ORB
- First breakout location
- Consolidation patterns

**Classification:**
- Objective: Direction and price levels can be measured
- Discretionary: Interpreting what direction means requires context

## 3. RETEST LOGIC (Mason's Entry Preference)

### **Mason Does NOT Enter Automatic Breakouts**

He prefers **retests** of key levels.

**Examples:**
- Price breaks above ORB High, then retest ORB High (acceptance test)
- Price breaks below ORB Low, then retest ORB Low (acceptance test)
- Price breaks above ORB Midpoint, then retest Midpoint (acceptance test)

**Critical distinction:**
Mason is looking for **acceptance and reaction**, NOT a full breakdown of the level.

**What he does NOT want:**
- Price violates ORB level with weak volume (fake breakout)
- Price fully reverses through the level (loss of structure)
- Price closes back inside the range (rejection)

**What he DOES want:**
- Price violates level, pulls back, then holds and continues (acceptance)
- Consolidation just below/above level, then moves again (confirmed structure)

**Examples of good retest:**
- Price rallies above ORB High to 6690
- Pulls back to 6688 (just below ORB High at 6689)
- Bounces and continues higher (acceptance confirmed)

**Examples of bad retest (avoid entry):**
- Price rallies above ORB High to 6690
- Pulls back through ORB High to 6685 (breaks structure completely)
- This is rejection, not a valid retest

**Classification:**
- Objective: Retest can be defined (close > level then come back within X points)
- Discretionary: "Good consolidation" vs "bad breakdown" requires visual interpretation

## 4. CONFLUENCE PROCESS

When price reaches an area of interest, Mason stacks evidence.

He does NOT enter based on single confirmation.

He builds a case.

### **Fair Value Gaps (FVGs)**

Gaps where price has not traded recently:

**Types Mason tracks:**
- 5-minute FVG (up, down, internal)
- 5-minute IFVG (internal FVG)
- 1-minute FVG (up, down, internal)
- 1-minute IFVG (internal FVG)

**Purpose:** FVGs are imbalances. Price seeks to fill them.

**Application:**
- If price is approaching a 1-minute FVG, it's a potential reversal area
- If price is coming from a FVG, it may continue toward next structure
- Multiple FVGs aligned = stronger confluence

**NOT entry signal alone.** FVGs are confirmations AT important locations.

**Classification:**
- Objective: FVGs can be calculated algorithmically (gaps between candles)
- Measurable: Size and location of gaps
- Discretionary: Whether a gap "matters" depends on market context

---

### **VWAP (Volume Weighted Average Price)**

Mason uses VWAP as a **directional filter**, not a signal.

**Rules he observes:**
- **Longs:** Generally want price above VWAP (unless strong conviction otherwise)
- **Shorts:** Generally want price below VWAP (unless strong conviction otherwise)

**VWAP is permission, not entry trigger.**

**Not a hard stop.** If there's a strong confluence reason to trade against VWAP, he can.

**But default bias:** Align with VWAP.

**Classification:**
- Objective: VWAP line can be calculated precisely
- Discretionary: "Strong reason otherwise" to trade against VWAP requires judgment

---

### **EMA (Exponential Moving Average)**

EMA is secondary confirmation.

**Mason's approach:**
- NOT the primary reason for a trade
- More of a trend-alignment tool
- Helps confirm which direction is "easier"

**Examples:**
- Price above EMA(50) in uptrend = confirmation of bias
- Price below EMA(50) in downtrend = confirmation of bias

**Not a hard entry rule.** Missing EMA alignment doesn't disqualify a trade if other confluence is strong.

**Classification:**
- Objective: EMA is calculated precisely
- Discretionary: Whether it's "in uptrend" or "in downtrend" can be ambiguous at inflection points

---

### **Volume**

Volume matters for confirmation.

Mason pays attention to:

**Volume Expansion:**
- Does price move on increasing volume? (conviction)
- Does price move on decreasing volume? (lack of participation)

**Participation:**
- Is the move attracting buyers/sellers?
- Or is it moving on thin participation?

**Reactions at Levels:**
- When price reaches a key level, what does volume do?
- Spike = interest
- Drying up = disinterest

**Classification:**
- Objective: Volume can be measured precisely
- Discretionary: Interpreting what volume "means" requires context (expansion vs normal, participation vs thin)

## 5. LIQUIDITY FRAMEWORK (Core of Mason's Thinking)

This section describes how Mason understands WHY price moves.

It's the most important part of his framework.

### **The Key Question: Where Is Price Trying to Go?**

Sometimes the ORB works perfectly and breaks cleanly.

Sometimes price completely ignores the ORB structure.

When that happens, Mason does NOT consider the trade invalid.

Instead he asks: **"What liquidity is price seeking?"**

### **Liquidity Targets Mason Tracks**

**Asia Session Levels:**
- Asia high = potential upper liquidity target
- Asia low = potential lower liquidity target

**London Session Levels:**
- London high = potential upper liquidity target
- London low = potential lower liquidity target

**Previous Day Levels:**
- Previous day high = potential upper liquidity target
- Previous day low = potential lower liquidity target

**NY Opening Level:**
- How far from previous close? (gap)
- This sets initial "fair value" for the day

### **Liquidity Draws (Why Price Moves)**

**Example 1: Seeking Asia Highs**
- Price breaks ORB High
- BUT it doesn't stop there
- It continues to Asia High level
- This was the real target, not the ORB breakout

**Example 2: Seeking Previous Day Low**
- Price breaks ORB Low
- But the ORB Low was only a waypoint
- Price continues to Previous Day Low
- The ORB was validating bias, not the target

**Example 3: Liquidity Rejection**
- Price rallies toward Asia High
- Doesn't reach it
- Reverses hard (liquidity grab and exit)
- This was a failed liquidity draw, not a breakout continuation

### **Mason's Mental Model**

The ORB is not the entire map.

The ORB exists INSIDE a larger market structure.

Bigger picture:
1. Asia/London sessions created structure
2. 8:00–8:15 ORB created local reference
3. Pre-NY behavior indicated bias direction
4. NY open provides entry opportunity
5. BUT: The real moves are often to deeper liquidity targets

**Questions Mason Asks Repeatedly:**
- "Did we break the ORB because we're targeting Asia highs?"
- "Did we break the ORB because we're targeting London lows?"
- "Did we sweep liquidity and reject?" (false breakout)
- "Did we take previous day highs and reverse?" (reversal trade)
- "Did we take previous day lows and reverse?" (reversal trade)
- "Is price seeking liquidity or accepting above it?" (is the move real?)

**Classification:**
- Objective: All liquidity targets (Asia H/L, London H/L, Previous Day H/L) can be measured
- Discretionary: Which target is price likely seeking depends on context and volume pattern
- Measurable: Correlation between ORB breakout direction and direction to actual liquidity target

## 6. MASON'S STEP-BY-STEP DECISION TREE

When Mason opens the market he follows this process:

### **Step 1: Establish Structure (Before 8:00)**
1. Plot Asia highs/lows
2. Plot London highs/lows
3. Plot Previous Day highs/lows
4. Note any gaps at open
5. Determine: "Where is price starting relative to recent structure?"

### **Step 2: Mark 8:00–8:15 ORB**
1. Track high and low from 8:00–8:15
2. Calculate midpoint
3. Note: Where did first trades go? Direction?
4. Determine: "What was the initial participation direction?"

### **Step 3: Watch 8:15–9:30**
1. Does price hold above/below ORB? (structure acceptance)
2. Does price consolidate near ORB? (indecision)
3. Does price move toward liquidity targets? (seeking)
4. Determine: "What bias is emerging before NY opens?"

### **Step 4: Evaluate NY Open (9:30)**
1. Note gap from pre-market close to NY open price
2. Note volume at open
3. Determine: "How aggressive is the open? Confirmation or reversal?"

### **Step 5: Look for Retest**
1. Wait for a pullback toward ORB level (High, Low, or Midpoint)
2. Does volume support retest? (tape reading)
3. Does retest accept the level or reject?
4. Determine: "Is this a valid retest or a false breakout?"

### **Step 6: Build Confluence**
At retest point, ask:
1. Is price above or below VWAP? (filter)
2. Is price above or below EMA? (trend alignment)
3. Are there FVGs at this level? (imbalance support)
4. Is volume expanding? (participation)
5. Is there a liquidity target beyond this level? (next move)
6. Determine: "Do I have enough confluence to enter?"

### **Step 7: Enter or Pass**
1. If confluence is strong: Enter with small size first (probe)
2. If confluence is weak: Pass and wait for clearer setup
3. If retest fails: Avoid (price rejected structure)

### **Step 8: Position Management**
1. Place initial SL below/above structure (NOT at arbitrary distance)
2. Scale into position as confluence increases
3. Move SL to breakeven after first TP hits
4. Trail as price moves toward liquidity target
5. Exit when reaching liquidity target or structure breaks

## 7. WHAT IS OBJECTIVE & MEASURABLE

These elements CAN be coded or backtested:

✅ **Measurable - Can be automated:**
- Session highs and lows (objective price levels)
- 8:00–8:15 ORB high and low (specific time range, objective)
- Pre-NY price direction (higher or lower than ORB?)
- Retest definition (price violates level, then comes back within X points)
- VWAP relationship (above or below?)
- EMA(50) relationship (above or below?)
- FVG identification (algorithmic: gaps between candles)
- Volume expansion detection (current bar vol > average vol?)
- Distance to liquidity targets (Asia H/L, London H/L, Prev Day H/L)

✅ **Measurable - Can be tracked:**
- Entry price and time
- Exit price and time
- Trade direction (long or short)
- Whether retest was used (yes or no)
- Number of confluence signals stacked
- Which liquidity target was hit (if any)
- Volume at entry
- Duration of trade

## 8. WHAT IS DISCRETIONARY

These elements CANNOT be easily automated:

🟡 **Requires Visual Interpretation:**
- Whether a retest is "acceptance" vs "rejection" (nuance in price action)
- Whether consolidation is "healthy" vs "concerning" (contextual)
- Whether a move has "conviction" (visual pattern reading)
- Tape reading (matching volume pattern to price action)
- "Strong reason otherwise" to trade against VWAP (context-dependent)
- Whether a liquidity draw is "real" or "fake" (probability judgment)
- When to scale in vs wait for more confirmation (risk management)
- When to move SL vs hold position (judgment call)

🟡 **Requires Market Context:**
- What does the broader market (NQ, SPY, indices) doing?
- What's the news/economic context?
- How is volatility? (affecting stop size)
- Is it a trend day or choppy day? (affects strategy)
- Are there market opens/closes affecting liquidity?

🟡 **Requires Judgment/Experience:**
- "This feels weak" (pattern recognition, intuition)
- "I don't like the setup" (combining signals into single judgment)
- "Better to wait" (patience, risk management)

## 9. DECISION FRAMEWORK CLASSIFICATION

Based on the above, Mason's system contains:

### **Fully Systematic (No discretion needed):**
- Session structure tracking (just plot the levels)
- 8:00–8:15 ORB calculation (measure the range)
- VWAP calculation (mathematical)
- EMA(50) calculation (mathematical)
- FVG identification (algorithmic)
- Volume expansion detection (numerical comparison)
- Distance to liquidity targets (math)

### **Rules with Execution Discretion (Framework + judgment):**
- Retest definition: "Close > level, then pullback ≤ X points before continuing"
  - Systematic: Define X (e.g., 2 points, or ½ of initial move)
  - Discretionary: What counts as "accepted"? (tape reading)

- Confluence threshold: "Need X signals before entry"
  - Systematic: Define X (e.g., 4 out of 5 signals)
  - Discretionary: Which signals matter most in current context?

- Liquidity targeting: "If price moves toward liquidity target Y, hold for it"
  - Systematic: Identify all liquidity targets
  - Discretionary: Which target is most likely? When to give up and exit?

### **Fully Discretionary (Cannot systematize):**
- "I don't like this setup" gut calls
- Tape reading nuance ("this volume looks weak")
- News/context impact on decision
- When to override the system ("make an exception")
- When to pass ("better to skip")

## 10. FRAMEWORK ASSESSMENT

### **Can This Be a Fully Automated Strategy?**

Partially.

**Would look like:**
- Plot all session levels automatically
- Calculate 8:00–8:15 ORB automatically
- Scan for retests automatically
- Stack confluence signals automatically
- Enter when threshold met (e.g., 4+ signals)
- Exit at liquidity targets or SL

**Limitations:**
- Can't interpret "acceptance vs rejection" perfectly (some false entries)
- Can't read tape (will miss weak-volume fakes)
- Can't adapt to market context (same rules in choppy day as trend day)
- Can't override on "feel" (will take some bad trades)

**Estimated performance:** ~50-60% win rate, lower profit factor due to automation limitations.

### **Can This Be a Hybrid (Discretionary + Rules)?**

Yes. Better fit for Mason's thinking.

**Would look like:**
- Automated: All measurement, all level plotting, all confluence stacking
- Automated: Alert when confluence threshold met
- Discretionary: Mason reviews alert, visual check on "acceptance", tape reading, market context
- Discretionary: Mason enters or skips
- Automated: Position management (SL, TP, trailing)

**Estimated performance:** ~60-70% win rate, higher profit factor due to discretion filtering.

### **Should This Remain Pure Discretionary?**

Yes, IF Mason has time to watch markets and discipline to follow process.

**Requirements:**
- Can't be automated because market context varies too much
- Tape reading is real skill, harder to systematize
- Decision-making requires judgment that changes intraday

**Estimated performance:** Variable, but ~65-75% win rate possible if Mason trades well, lower if emotional.
